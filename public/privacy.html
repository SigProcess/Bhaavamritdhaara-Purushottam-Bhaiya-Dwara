Privacy Policy

Bhaavamrit — Bhaavamritdhaara, पुरुषोत्तम भाईया द्वारा

Last updated: 9 November 2026

No accounts No advertising No developer analytics No payments Works offline

Bhaavamrit has no sign-up, no advertising, no payments, and no analytics, telemetry, or crash-reporting software of any kind. We do not operate a server that receives information about you, and we do not build a profile of you. What this policy will not do is pretend that nothing ever leaves your device: when you play a discourse or a bhajan video, playback happens inside YouTube's own embedded player, so Google receives that request. Section 5 says exactly who receives what, and when.

1What this policy covers

This single policy covers all three ways the project is published:

  • The Bhaavamrit iOS app (bundle identifier com.bhaavamrit.app), published on the App Store. It is built with Capacitor: the interface is the same devotional library, rendered in a web view inside the app, with native iOS code for reading and sharing PDF documents.
  • The Bhaavamrit Android app, published on Google Play. It is a Trusted Web Activity, which means Android opens the website in a full-screen Chrome-provided window belonging to the app.
  • The website at purushottambhaiya.netlify.app, which can also be installed as a progressive web app.

The project is non-commercial and devotional. It does not sell anything, does not monetise attention, and has nothing to gain from collecting data about you.

2What the app does not do

Each of the following is a statement about the published source code, not an aspiration:

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, or attribution software. There is no Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, Firebase, Crashlytics, Sentry, Mixpanel, Amplitude, PostHog, Plausible, Hotjar, Segment, Datadog, Bugsnag, or advertising-measurement library anywhere in the app. The entire runtime dependency list of the web app is React and Capacitor; the only native iOS dependency is Capacitor itself.
  • No tracking. The iOS privacy manifest declares no tracking, no tracking domains, and no collected data types. The app does not ask for the advertising identifier and does not use the App Tracking Transparency prompt, because it has nothing to track you with.
  • No accounts, logins, or profiles. There is no sign-in screen, no password, no social login, and no user record anywhere.
  • No payments, purchases, or subscriptions. There is no in-app purchase, no payment form, and no billing of any kind. Everything in the app is free.
  • No advertising. No ad network, no sponsored placement, no interstitial.
  • No developer-operated back end. The native iOS code makes no network requests at all; it reads documents that are already inside the app. There is no server of ours that logs your use of the app.
  • No contact, photo, microphone, camera, location, or health access. The iOS app declares no such permission, so iOS will never prompt you for one.

3What is stored on your device, and stays there

The app is built to work offline. That requires storing things locally. None of it is transmitted to us, because there is nowhere for it to be transmitted to.

Devotional content bundled inside the app

The iOS app ships with 135 bhajan PDFs, 3 books, and the full content catalogue compiled into the installed app, roughly 37 MB. They are present from the moment you install it and readable with no network connection whatsoever. Removing the app removes them.

Offline cache (website and installed web app)

On the website and the installable web app, a service worker stores a copy of the pages, styles, scripts, and images so the site opens without a connection. It also keeps a short-lived cache of video thumbnail images and of the web fonts the page uses. This cache lives in your browser's storage for this site and is cleared when you clear site data or uninstall the app.

Content catalogue storage

So that a content update can be validated before it is used, the app can keep catalogue versions in a local database on the device (an IndexedDB database named bhaavamrit-catalog-store in the browser; a folder inside the app's own storage on iOS). It holds devotional catalogue metadata only — titles, identifiers, and file references. It holds nothing about you. On iOS the app starts from the catalogue packaged inside it and is not configured to request a remote one.

Things that exist only while the app is open

What you type into the Bhajan Finder, which screen you are on, which video is selected, and whether the device is online are held in the app's memory while you use it and are gone when the app closes. Search words are matched against the packaged catalogue on your device; they are never sent anywhere.

What the app does not store

The app writes no cookies of its own, and uses neither localStorage nor sessionStorage. On iOS it writes nothing to UserDefaults and nothing to the keychain.

4Device permissions

  • iOS: none. The app declares no permission-usage descriptions at all, so iOS has nothing to ask you for.
  • Android: notifications only (POST_NOTIFICATIONS), used to let you know about new bhajan or discourse playlists. You can refuse it, or turn it off later in Android settings, and the app keeps working.

5Third parties that do receive something

This is the part that matters. We collect nothing, but the app is not sealed off from the internet, and we are not going to imply otherwise. Each provider below is governed by its own privacy policy, not by ours, and we have no access to and no control over what it keeps.

YouTube and Google — video playback and thumbnails

Discourses and bhajan videos are not hosted by us. They play through YouTube's embedded player. When you open a playlist or start a video, your device loads YouTube's player code and video stream directly from Google, and video thumbnail images load from img.youtube.com. As a result Google receives your IP address, information about your device and software, and the identifier of the video or playlist concerned, and may set cookies or similar identifiers on your device, depending on your device and Google account settings. If you are signed in to a Google account, Google may associate that activity with your account. If you choose to open a video in the YouTube app or website, you leave Bhaavamrit entirely and YouTube's own terms apply.

Google's practices are described in the Google Privacy Policy, and playback is subject to the YouTube Terms of Service.

Google Fonts — typography

The website and the app's interface request their typefaces from fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com, so Google receives a request from your device when those fonts are fetched. Once cached, they are served locally. This policy page itself requests no fonts from anywhere. See the Google Privacy Policy.

Netlify — hosting

The website, the page you are reading, and the content the Android app displays are hosted by Netlify, which keeps ordinary web-server records such as IP address, requested URL, referrer, device and browser details, and the date and time. We do not add any tracking on top of that, and we do not use those records to identify or profile visitors. The iOS app's own content is read from inside the installed app, so simply opening and reading in the iOS app does not involve Netlify. See the Netlify Privacy Statement.

Apple and Google Play — distribution

Installing and updating the app happens through the App Store or Google Play, and those stores record that activity under their own policies. We receive only the aggregate, anonymous statistics those stores choose to show developers; we cannot identify an individual user from them. See the Apple Privacy Policy and the Google Privacy Policy.

6Reading and sharing documents

On iOS, opening a bhajan or a book opens Apple's own PDF reader inside the app, reading a file that is already installed on your device. Nothing is downloaded and nothing is uploaded. The app can only open documents that are listed in its own catalogue and stored in its own folders; it cannot reach your files.

If you tap Export, iOS presents the standard share sheet and you choose where the PDF goes — Messages, Mail, Files, another app, a printer. That only happens when you ask for it, the file is the local devotional PDF, and whatever you pick is governed by that destination's own privacy practices. No copy is sent to us.

7Children

The app is devotional content suitable for all ages and is not directed at children in particular. We knowingly collect no personal information from anyone, children included, because we collect none at all. Note that videos play through YouTube's embedded player, so a parent who wants to limit what Google receives should apply the relevant Google account and device controls.

8Your data, and your choices

Because we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to export, correct, or delete at your request — and no record we could use to recognise you if you asked. Your practical controls are these:

  • Remove everything the app stored: uninstall the app, or clear this site's data in your browser.
  • Avoid YouTube and Google entirely: read the bhajans and books, which work fully offline, and do not open a video.
  • Control what Google retains: use your Google account controls and your browser's or device's cookie settings.
  • Turn off Android notifications: in Android settings, at any time.

9Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, the policy is updated and the “Last updated” date at the top changes with it. The same text is kept in the project's public repository as PRIVACY_POLICY.md, so the two always agree.
